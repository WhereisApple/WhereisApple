# 💫 About Me:
I'm a final-year Computer Science student who is probably a little too curious about how things work. I don't really like just using something without understanding what's happening underneath it, which is probably why I've ended up getting interested in everything from web development and AI to operating systems, assembly, networking, cybersecurity, and even how a computer actually boots.<br><br>Right now, I'm mainly focused on becoming a strong software engineer. I've worked with C, Python, JavaScript, HTML, CSS, PHP, MySQL, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, pandas, and a bunch of other technologies. I've also been learning Android development with Kotlin and exploring AWS and security. I'm still figuring out exactly where I want to specialize, but software development, system design, security, and low-level computing are the areas that interest me the most.<br><br>One of the things I'm currently really interested in is building my own operating system. I'm learning how computers work from the ground up — BIOS, UEFI, bootloaders, GRUB, kernels, memory, CPU registers, interrupts, assembly, C, filesystems, processes, and everything in between. I've been experimenting with x86 assembly and QEMU, and eventually I want to build an OS that I can actually boot alongside Windows and Linux. I like projects where I have to understand the fundamentals instead of just importing a library and making something work.<br><br>I also enjoy building random projects just because an idea sounds interesting. I've worked on things involving AI, sentiment/perception analysis, recommendation systems, football prediction, budgeting, coding platforms, social platforms, weather data, and Android widgets. One of my current ideas involves analysing how people perceive government policies over time using historical data, NLP, transformers, and sentence embeddings. Another project I've worked on is a coding platform with a dark hacker-style interface, using FastAPI, databases, authentication, and the Monaco editor. I also want to eventually build a malware-analysis sandbox and learn cybersecurity and networking properly rather than just learning security tools without understanding the systems behind them.<br><br>Outside academics, I'm really into badminton as well as football. I've played football at university level and enjoy playing badminton from a learning perspective.<br><br>I'm also interested in fitness and becoming a hybrid athlete. I want to build strength, endurance, mobility, and calisthenics skills rather than focusing only on how I look. Long term, I'd like to get into things like Hyrox and eventually an Ironman. I'm also interested in boxing and Muay Thai and have been trying to learn more about them.<br><br>Gaming is another big part of me. I like Minecraft, especially modded Minecraft, exploration, new dimensions, structures, magic, weapons, and systems that let you create your own experience. I'm also interested in game development and want to properly learn Unity and C#. Eventually I'd love to make my own games, especially ones with strong worldbuilding and interesting mechanics. I like games such as Hollow Knight because of the way the world itself tells a story.<br><br>I also have a weird fascination with making physical things interact with software. For example, I've thought about making a Unity FPS where a modified Nerf gun acts as the controller, including shooting, reloading, and movement. I've also had ideas around games, custom hardware, operating systems, and other projects that are probably unnecessarily complicated for a student to attempt — which is exactly why I want to attempt them.<br><br>I enjoy learning things from the absolute basics and then going deeper. If I'm learning something like operating systems, I don't just want to know what a kernel is. I want to understand what happens from the moment I press the power button, how firmware finds something to boot, how the bootloader works, how the CPU enters the right mode, how the kernel gets loaded, how memory is managed, how processes are created, and eventually how the whole system comes together.<br><br>The same applies to programming. I don't want to only know how to solve a DSA problem. I want to understand why the solution works, what happens in memory, what the complexity actually means, and how I could implement the underlying structure myself.<br><br>I'm also someone who likes experimenting. I'll probably spend a few days learning assembly, then get distracted by OS development, then decide I should learn networking, then start thinking about a malware sandbox, and somehow end up working on a game in Unity. I don't always follow a perfectly linear path, but most of the things I explore eventually connect back to my main interest: understanding technology deeply and building things with it.<br><br>Career-wise, I'm currently focused on getting strong software engineering experience after graduation. I want to become genuinely good at building systems rather than just collecting technologies for my resume. Eventually, I'd like to work on more difficult engineering problems, explore security and systems more deeply, and hopefully work internationally.<br><br>More than anything, I like the feeling of taking an idea that exists only in my head and slowly turning it into something that actually runs. Whether that's a website, an AI model, a game, an operating system, a piece of hardware, or some completely unnecessary experiment, that's probably the part of technology I enjoy the most.<br>
